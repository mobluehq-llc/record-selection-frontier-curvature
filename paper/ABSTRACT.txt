An upward bend in the best-so-far curve of AI progress is read as acceleration. We ask whether it can appear with no acceleration, because a record-setting model is selected partly for favourable noise. On the release dates and design of the public METR time-horizon data (33 models, 170 tasks) we simulate exactly steady exponential progress. A quadratic fitted to the record-setting models then bends upward on average as much as a trend whose growth rate rises 1.76-fold across the window; the fit through all models shows no such bias. The direction depends on the calendar: the bias was downward on 6 of 7 alternative schedules, and when we planted a known upward bend into date-permuted scores of 22 public AI benchmarks, the mean record-only curvature bent downward in 20 of them. Standard inference neither controls false alarms nor has high power: the usual two-sided F test on the records, applied to date-permuted benchmark series without a planted trend, was oversized in 18 of 22 series (14 of 22 counting undefined fits as non-rejections), while a size-valid robust test on all models had 34% power against a doubled growth rate. In the observed series the bend of the record curve is compatible with constant growth (one-sided p = 0.24). The all-model curvature is positive (one-sided HC3 p = 0.0058, models treated as independent), but its evidence weakens when an early model is omitted, two stand-ins are redated or dropped, or developer clustering is allowed (redating raises the estimate) (Webb cluster-bootstrap p = 0.094). We do not claim that AI progress is or is not accelerating. Only the first size tests of the record-only fit and the two-sided benchmark audit are confirmatory; all else is exploratory (the bias, calendar and corrected-test results were pre-registered).
