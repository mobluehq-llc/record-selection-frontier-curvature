AI capability trends are often summarised by the best-performing model at each date, and an upward bend in that best-so-far curve is read as acceleration. Using the release dates and evaluation structure of the public METR time-horizon data, version 1.0 (33 models, 170 tasks), we simulate steady exponential progress. A quadratic fitted to the record-setting models then has a mean curvature equivalent to a 1.76-fold rise in the growth rate across the window: on this calendar, record selection creates spurious upward curvature. On other release calendars the bias is mostly downward (negative on 6 of 7 alternatives), and after date permutation and trend planting the mean fitted record curvature reversed sign in 20 of 22 benchmark series. Among the eleven record-based procedures with reported size-adjusted power, the maximum was 27.9% against a doubling of the growth rate; a heteroskedasticity-robust test on all models reached 34%. In the observed series the curvature of the record heights is compatible with constant growth (one-sided p = 0.24). The all-model curvature is positive (one-sided HC3 p = 0.0058, treating models as independent). The strength of evidence is sensitive to omitting either early anchor, to how the two proxies are dated or whether they are included, and to accounting for developer clustering (Webb cluster-bootstrap p = 0.094). A fixed September 2024 break fits about as well as the quadratic. Among date permutations with a defined fit, the naive two-sided record test was oversized in 18 of 22 series. Counting undefined fits as non-rejections gives 14 of 22. Reporting every evaluated model, and calibrating tests to the release calendar, would make claims about changing rates of progress easier to interpret.
